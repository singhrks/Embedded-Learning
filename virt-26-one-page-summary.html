One page: the whole track, for revision

Twenty-five chapters compressed to the facts that change how you read everything else. If a line here does not immediately make sense, that is the chapter to reopen.

The whole thing, in one page

The track is one long answer to a single question — how does a machine convincingly pretend to be several — and the answer has a definite structure worth being able to recite.

   A HYPERVISOR IS A PROGRAM THAT MAKES OTHER OPERATING
   SYSTEMS BELIEVE THEY OWN THE HARDWARE.

   IT NEEDS FOUR THINGS, AND THE TRACK IS THOSE FOUR:

   1. A PLACE TO STAND, more privileged than the guest
        EL2, and a run loop that enters the guest and
        handles what comes back.                (03, 08, 09)

   2. CONTROL OVER MEMORY
        Stage 2: a second translation the guest cannot see
        or edit. The guest's "physical" addresses are
        intermediate; the hypervisor decides where they
        land.                                   (05, 06, 07)

   3. CONTROL OVER WHAT THE GUEST MAY DO DIRECTLY
        A trap policy. Everything trapped is correct and
        slow; everything untrapped is fast and must be
        safe. This one trade shapes every design.    (04)

   4. DEVICES
        Emulate them (correct, slow), share them with a
        cooperative guest (virtio — fast), or hand one over
        (passthrough — fastest, least flexible).
                                          (14, 15, 16, 17)

   INTERRUPTS AND TIME are special cases of 4 that need
   their own hardware support, because emulating them
   naively costs more than everything else combined.
                                                  (11, 12, 13)

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   AND THE ONE SENTENCE THAT EXPLAINS MOST DESIGN CHOICES:

     EVERY EXIT IS EXPENSIVE, SO THE WHOLE FIELD IS ABOUT
     NOT HAVING ONE.

   virtio, VHE, direct injection, the KV cache of vGIC list
   registers, posted interrupts, WFI handling, stage-2
   block mappings - every one is an exit that someone
   removed.

Approximate figures that let you sanity-check a design or a complaint without measuring first.

Misconceptions this track should have removed

Each of these is common enough to be worth checking yourself against explicitly.

The beliefWhat is actually trueCh.
A hypervisor emulates the CPUThe guest runs natively on the real CPU. Only the things that would break isolation trap02
Virtualisation overhead is CPU overheadIt is almost always exits, memory translation, or scheduling — rarely the instructions23
Guest physical addresses are physicalThey are intermediate. Stage 2 decides where they land05
More trapping is saferMore trapping is slower; safety comes from trapping the right things04
virtio is a deviceIt is a shared-memory transport with a device protocol on top15
Passthrough is strictly betterIt costs you migration, overcommit, and pinned memory17
A VM is a strong security boundaryGood against a compromised workload; the VMM's device models are a large surface25
Partitioning gives complete isolationCaches, memory bandwidth and interconnect are still shared19
Confidential computing protects availabilityConfidentiality and integrity only — the host can always refuse to run you21
The hypervisor sets the guest's memory typeThe two stages combine, and the more restrictive wins07

Where to go from here

The track stops where the mechanisms are solid. These are the directions that reward the next stretch of effort.

   WRITE THE TWO-HUNDRED-LINE HYPERVISOR from chapter 18.
   Nothing else converts this material into understanding as
   quickly, and it fits in an afternoon.

   THEN, DEPENDING ON WHERE YOU ARE HEADED:

     AUTOMOTIVE / SAFETY
       chapters 19 and 21. The interesting constraints are
       freedom from interference and certification, and
       both push toward partitioning rather than sharing.

     CLOUD / SERVER
       chapters 18, 23 and 10. Read a real VMM's source,
       and learn the x86 vocabulary - most of the
       literature assumes it.

     SECURITY
       chapters 20, 21 and 25, then the isolation and TEE
       tracks. The recurring question is which component is
       in the trusted set, and every design on this site is
       an answer to it.

     EMBEDDED / FIRMWARE
       chapters 19 and 20, then the MCU and ARM tracks.

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   AND THE THING WORTH KEEPING IN PROPORTION: virtualisation
   is a set of mechanisms for controlling what software can
   see and do. Every one of them - a second translation
   stage, a trap policy, a shared-memory transport, an
   attested boundary - appears elsewhere on this site under
   another name. Recognising that is worth more than any
   single register.
