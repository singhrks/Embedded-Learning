Prompting and in-context learning

The model's weights are frozen. Everything you can change is the text you put in front of it — and that text is not an incantation, it is a conditioning signal on a next-token distribution. Understanding it mechanically is what separates prompt engineering from superstition.

What a prompt actually does

Almost every piece of prompting advice is downstream of one fact from chapter 23: the model computes a distribution over the next token given everything before it. A prompt does not instruct the model. It moves the conditioning.

   THE MODEL COMPUTES:   P(next token | everything so far)

   THE PROMPT IS "everything so far". That is the whole
   mechanism. There is no separate instruction channel.

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   WHICH EXPLAINS THINGS THAT OTHERWISE LOOK LIKE MAGIC:

   · "You are an expert C programmer" helps, not because the
     model believes it, but because text that FOLLOWS such a
     sentence in the training corpus is different from text
     that follows nothing. You are selecting a region of the
     distribution.

   · FEW-SHOT EXAMPLES work for the same reason. After three
     input/output pairs in a fixed format, the highest-
     probability continuation is a fourth pair in that
     format. You are not teaching; you are establishing a
     pattern that is cheapest to continue.

   · "Do not mention X" often INCREASES mentions of X. The
     tokens for X are now in the context, raising their
     probability. Describe what you want, not what you
     forbid.

   · The model cannot know what it was not given. "Use the
     latest version" conditions on nothing if the latest
     version is not in the context or the weights.

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   IN-CONTEXT LEARNING is the name for the fact that examples
   in the prompt change behaviour WITHOUT changing weights.
   Nothing is learned in the gradient-descent sense; the
   forward pass simply conditions on what it was shown.

   THE TEST THAT MAKES THIS CONCRETE: give few-shot examples
   with DELIBERATELY WRONG labels but a consistent format.
   Performance usually degrades far less than you would
   expect - which says the format is carrying much of the
   benefit, not the correctness of the answers.

The techniques that reliably help

Prompting has accumulated a great deal of folklore. These are the handful of techniques with a clear mechanism behind them, which is also why they keep working as models change.

TechniqueWhat it doesWhy it works
Be specific about the output formatState the shape you want, ideally with an exampleRemoves the model's need to guess a format, which is otherwise sampled like anything else
Few-shot examplesTwo to five input/output pairsEstablishes a pattern the continuation can follow; carries format far better than prose describing the format
Let it work step by stepAsk for reasoning before the answerEach token is one forward pass; intermediate tokens are the only place multi-step computation can happen
Put instructions near the endLong context first, the task lastModels attend unevenly across a long context; recency is usually favoured
Give it an out"If the answer is not in the text, say so"Without permission to decline, every continuation is an answer — and an answer must be invented
DecomposeSeveral focused calls rather than one large oneEach call has a simpler distribution to hit; failures become localisable
Why "think step by step" is not a trick

A transformer does a fixed amount of computation per token — one forward pass through a fixed number of layers. There is no loop, and no way to "think longer" about a single token. So if a problem needs five sequential steps, the only place those steps can happen is across five tokens' worth of generated output. Asking for reasoning before the answer is not flattering the model into trying harder; it is allocating it more computation. This also tells you when it will not help: a task that needs no sequential steps gains nothing, and a model asked to reason after stating its answer gains nothing either, because the answer token was already sampled.

Structuring a prompt you will have to maintain

A prompt that works once and a prompt you can keep working are different artefacts. The second one has a structure, and the structure exists so that you can change one part without disturbing the others.

# The layout that survives contact with real use.

[ROLE / CONTEXT]        who is speaking, what the situation is
                        - stable, rarely edited

[TASK]                  what to do, in one or two sentences
                        - the thing you actually iterate on

[CONSTRAINTS]           format, length, what to do when unsure
                        - grows as you discover failure modes

[EXAMPLES]              two to five, covering the EDGE cases
                        rather than the obvious ones
                        - the highest-value part to invest in

[INPUT]                 the actual data, clearly delimited
                        - LAST, and unambiguously separated

---------------------------------------------------------

The sampling parameters, revisited

Chapter 25 covered how sampling works. This is the practical half: which parameter to reach for, and the one that is almost always the wrong lever.

   TEMPERATURE scales the logits before the softmax.

     low  (0 - 0.3)   near-deterministic. Extraction,
                      classification, code, anything with a
                      correct answer.
     mid  (0.5 - 0.8) default for prose and conversation.
     high (1.0+)      genuine variety, and genuine nonsense.

   TOP-P (nucleus) keeps the smallest set of tokens whose
   probability sums to p, then renormalises. It adapts:
   where the model is confident the set is tiny, where it is
   unsure the set is large.

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   THE PRACTICAL RULE: change ONE of temperature or top-p,
   not both. Interacting them makes behaviour very hard to
   reason about, and the effect of each becomes untestable.

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   AND THE MISTAKE THAT WASTES THE MOST TIME:

     Turning the temperature DOWN to fix a factually wrong
     answer.

   Temperature 0 does not make the model correct; it makes it
   CONSISTENTLY produce its single most likely output. If the
   most likely output is wrong, you have removed your only
   chance of getting a right one. A wrong answer is a
   knowledge or context problem - which means retrieval
   (chapter 29), not a sampling parameter.

   THE ONE PLACE LOW TEMPERATURE GENUINELY HELPS is
   reproducibility, which matters enormously when you are
   trying to test anything (chapter 31). Even then it is
   rarely bit-exact - batching and floating-point
   non-determinism on a GPU mean identical inputs can still
   diverge.

Iterating on a prompt without fooling yourself

The failure mode here is specific and very common: you change a prompt, the example you were staring at gets better, and you never notice the three cases it broke. The discipline that prevents it is unglamorous.

   1. COLLECT FAILURES BEFORE EDITING. Twenty real inputs
      with their correct outputs. Twenty is enough to be
      informative and small enough that you will actually do
      it.

   2. CHANGE ONE THING.

   3. RUN THE WHOLE SET, not the case that annoyed you.

   4. RECORD the score AND the prompt version together.

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   WITHOUT THIS you are doing gradient descent by hand with
   a batch size of one, on a noisy objective, with no
   memory. It feels productive and it moves in circles -
   most people have a prompt that is worse than one they had
   two days ago and no way to know it.

   KEEP PROMPTS IN VERSION CONTROL, as files rather than
   string literals buried in code. They are behaviour. They
   deserve diffs and review like anything else that is.

   ---------------------------------------------------------

   AND KNOW WHEN TO STOP PROMPTING:

     the model lacks the FACTS       -> retrieval (ch. 29)
     the model needs to ACT          -> tools    (ch. 30)
     the format is unreliable        -> constrained decoding
                                        or a schema
     it fails on a whole CATEGORY    -> fine-tuning (ch. 24)
     you are on prompt version 30    -> the task is
                                        underspecified; go
                                        and define it
