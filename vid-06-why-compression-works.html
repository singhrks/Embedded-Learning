Why compression works

Raw 4K60 video is 12 gigabits per second. A streaming service delivers the same picture in 15 megabits — a ratio of 800:1. That is not clever packing; it is the systematic removal of four different kinds of redundancy, three of which you never notice.

The problem, in numbers

/* Uncompressed video is not slightly too big. It is
   impossible.                                             */

  bitrate = width * height * bpp * fps

  1920x1080  4:2:0 8-bit  30 fps
        1920 * 1080 * 1.5 * 8 * 30      =  747 Mbit/s
  3840x2160  4:2:0 10-bit 60 fps
        3840 * 2160 * 1.5 * 10 * 60     =  7.5 Gbit/s
  3840x2160  4:4:4 12-bit 60 fps
        3840 * 2160 * 3   * 12 * 60     = 17.9 Gbit/s

  ONE HOUR of the middle one: 3.4 TERABYTES.
  Delivered at 15 Mbit/s: 6.75 GB. A ratio of about 500:1.

  FOR COMPARISON
    a good LOSSLESS image compressor    ~2:1
    lossless video (FFV1, lossless x264) ~2-3:1

  So lossless gets you 2x and we need 500x. Everything
  interesting in this chapter is about the other 250x, and all
  of it is DISCARDING INFORMATION.

The four redundancies

   1. SPATIAL - neighbouring pixels are similar
        A photograph is mostly smooth. Knowing a pixel tells
        you a lot about the one beside it.
        EXPLOITED BY: intra prediction (predict from
        neighbours) and the transform (concentrate energy into
        a few coefficients).                    Chapters 7, 11

   2. TEMPORAL - consecutive frames are nearly identical
        At 30 fps, 33 ms apart, most of the frame did not
        change, and what did change mostly MOVED.
        EXPLOITED BY: motion compensation - describe this
        block as "that block over there, shifted".
        THE BIGGEST WIN BY FAR.                    Chapter 9

   3. STATISTICAL - symbols are not equally likely
        After prediction, most residuals are near zero. Give
        the common values short codes.
        EXPLOITED BY: entropy coding.               Chapter 8
        This is the only one that is LOSSLESS.

   4. PERCEPTUAL - the eye does not see everything
        Less acuity for colour than brightness. Less for high
        spatial frequencies. Less next to a strong edge, and
        for a moment after a scene cut.
        EXPLOITED BY: chroma subsampling (chapter 3) and
        QUANTISATION (chapter 7).
        THIS IS WHERE THE LOSS HAPPENS, deliberately.

   THE STRUCTURE OF EVERY CODEC SINCE 1990

     PREDICT   -> guess the block from what the decoder
                  already has (neighbours, or an earlier
                  frame)
     TRANSFORM -> convert the prediction ERROR to frequency,
                  concentrating its energy
     QUANTISE  -> divide and round. THE LOSSY STEP.
     ENTROPY   -> code the result losslessly

   and the decoder runs it backwards. H.261 in 1988 and AV1 in
   2018 differ in how well each stage is done, not in the
   structure.
Quantisation is the only lossy step

Prediction, transform and entropy coding are all reversible in principle. It is quantisation — dividing coefficients by a step size and rounding — that throws information away, and it is the only place that happens. That is why one number, the quantisation parameter, controls quality and bitrate throughout every codec, and why "QP" is the number you actually tune.

The theoretical floor

/* Shannon: you cannot losslessly code a source below its
   ENTROPY.

     H = - sum over symbols of  p(s) * log2 p(s)   bits/symbol

   A source where every one of 256 values is equally likely
   has H = 8 bits. Nothing can compress it. Random data does
   not compress - not because compressors are weak, but
   because there is nothing there.

   A source where 0 occurs 90% of the time:
     H = -(0.9*log2(0.9) + 0.1*log2(0.1/255)*255...) ~ 0.9 bits

   So the whole game is: TRANSFORM THE DATA UNTIL ITS ENTROPY
   IS LOW, then code near that floor.

   Prediction does exactly that. Raw pixel values are roughly
   uniform over their range. Prediction RESIDUALS - the error
   after guessing - cluster hard around zero:

     raw pixel values      residuals after prediction
     .....:::::::.....           :
     ...:::::::::::...          :::
     .:::::::::::::::.        ..:::..
     0            255        -50  0  +50

   Same information, far lower entropy. The entropy coder then
   gets close to that floor (chapter 8).                   */

Rate-distortion: the actual decision

   An encoder is not "compressing". It is repeatedly choosing
   between options that trade BITS against ERROR.

     Should this block be intra or inter coded?
     Which of 35 intra directions?
     Split into four sub-blocks, or not?
     Which reference frame? Which motion vector?
     What quantisation step?

   Each choice costs some RATE (bits) and causes some
   DISTORTION (error). You cannot minimise both.

   THE LAGRANGIAN, and this one equation is the heart of every
   modern encoder:

        J = D + lambda * R

     D  distortion - usually SSD (sum of squared differences)
                     or SATD (transformed absolute differences)
     R  rate - the actual bits this choice costs
     lambda  the exchange rate between them, derived from QP

   The encoder tries the options, computes J for each, and
   picks the smallest. That is RATE-DISTORTION OPTIMISATION,
   and it is why encoding is 10-100x slower than decoding.

        distortion
           |*
           | *
           |  *.
           |    *..              the RD CURVE
           |       *....
           |           *.......
           |                  *..........
           +------------------------------- rate

   A BETTER CODEC is one whose curve sits lower and left: the
   same quality for fewer bits. "AV1 is 30% better than H.264"
   means its curve is 30% left at equal quality - measured
   with BD-rate, which integrates the gap between two curves.

   AND THE PRESET DIAL: "veryslow" searches more options and
   lands closer to the true optimum. "ultrafast" tries a
   handful. Same codec, same decoder, 2-3x difference in
   bitrate for the same quality - which is why encoder
   QUALITY is mostly a search problem, not a format one.

#     filters do this reliably.
#   * ALWAYS LOOK AT THE VIDEO. A metric tells you where to

Compression artefacts are not random noise

Each has a signature that names its cause. Blocking — visible square edges — is coarse quantisation plus a block transform. Ringing / mosquito noise — shimmer around sharp edges — is quantising away high-frequency coefficients (Gibbs). Banding in gradients is quantisation plus insufficient bit depth. Smearing is motion compensation failing to find a match. Pumping — quality visibly oscillating — is rate control. Recognising the artefact tells you which knob to turn.
